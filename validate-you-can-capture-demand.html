Validate You Can Capture Demand

Before you invest in building a product, prove that you can capture demand for it. Capturing demand means qualified people become aware of you, click through to your landing page, and sign up.

Building has never been easier. Distribution is still hard, and left unsolved, it becomes a failure condition. Many startups die not because the product was bad, but because they couldn't find a way to acquire customers profitably. Validating you can demand capture early is how you start to de-risk that outcome.

You don't need a product to do this

Here's the part most founders miss. You don't need a functional product to start validating this. Willingness to pay can be demonstrated long before there's anything to pay for. A landing page, a signup flow, and a few well-designed questions are enough to run the entire experiment.

The two things you're actually validating

  1. The channel can work economically. There exists a channel where you can reach your ICP at a cost that could plausibly support the business. This means a cost per qualified signup that, projected forward, leaves room for a real margin. Not just "we got some clicks."
  2. Your team can work the channel. A channel that works in theory can still fail in your hands. This is sometimes called founder-channel fit. Cold outbound is a bad bet for a team that dreads picking up the phone. SEO is a bad bet for a team that needs customers this quarter. The question isn't just "can this product be sold this way?" It's "can we sell it this way?"

The stage that makes this an experiment is the last one: qualification. To be clear, qualifying purchasing intent does not mean taking anyone's money. You're measuring whether they would pay, not making them pay.

There are plenty of ways to capture that signal without a dollar changing hands such as a click through to a pricing page, a booked sales call, an answer to an intent-qualifying question during signup.

We picked the last one. Onboarding questions gave us a signal on every signup, not just the few who wander to a pricing page. We asked five of them, designed to learn who the user was, what they needed, and whether they would pay.

The five qualification questions

With qualification in place, the key metric becomes cost per qualified signup. Take your ad spend and divide it by only the signups that matched your ICP and showed purchasing intent. Everyone else gets filtered out.

To be clear about what this number is not: it is not your cost of customer acquisition. You won't know your true CAC until people are actually paying. But it lets you make reasonable estimates. If your cost per qualified signup is $200 and your price point is $100, you don't need to wait for more data. That channel is not going to work.

From here, there are two outcomes.

  1. The numbers are unlikely to work. You either need a new channel for the same idea, or a new idea. Painful, but better than learning this after you built an entire product.
  2. The numbers look plausible. The channel produces qualified leads at a cost that could support the business. You can move forward to the next step of the playbook.

Why now instead of later?

You might object, "won't we have to do all this anyway once the product is built?" Yes, and that's exactly the argument for doing it first. If you build the product and then discover a channel full of qualified leads that your product doesn't quite fit, your budget is already sunk. You have no flexibility left.

Run the experiment first, and the order works in your favour. Once you find a channel of qualified leads, you can tailor the product to those leads, giving you the best chance of making the business work.
