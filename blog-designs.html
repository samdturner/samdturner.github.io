Article design directions

Three high-fidelity takes on the blog's reading experience. Same sample content in each, so you are comparing type, colour, and weight. Pay attention to how the From our sprint and Pro tip blocks are treated: each aims to feel like a designed insert worth stopping for.

Design 1 · Editorial serif

Literary and authoritative, in the spirit of a print magazine. Fraunces display headings over a Newsreader serif body on warm ivory. Callouts are restrained sidebars: a hairline rule, a small-caps label, and italic serif text that reads like a considered aside.

The weekly sprint that won our first customers

For most SaaS businesses the risk isn't whether it can be built. It's running out of time, money, or motivation before you learn what to build.

Day 2: Synthesize and prioritize

By Tuesday, the weekend's data and Monday's interviews are in. We pull the signal onto one board, dot-vote the takeaways, and pick a single focus for the week.

From our sprint

Users kept telling us the feedback was why they'd pay, but they couldn't tell what each score meant. We bet a clearer feedback screen would lift second attempts, and it did.

Prioritization isn't about evaluating every idea. It's about agreeing, quickly, on the one bet worth making this week.

Pro tip

Keep your engineer in the room. When they can price a change on the spot, prioritization stops being guesswork and the whole loop speeds up.

Design 2 · Modern product sans

Crisp and current, in the spirit of a well-designed product blog. All Inter, tight headings, indigo accent on clean white. Callouts become rounded, tinted cards with an icon badge and a bold label, so they clearly read as intentional highlight blocks.

Design 3 · Bold & confident (refined)

Assertive and modern: Space Grotesk display headings, high-contrast near-black text, a single deep-teal accent. Callouts use the restrained left-rule treatment from Design 1 (accent rule, small-caps label, icon) but in clean upright body text, so they feel considered and distinct without shouting.
